# Headline (max 110 characters)

Identify the technical contribution and its domain or operating regime. Never
use a fullstop at the end of this title.

**Source paper:** {citation}

## Technical Problem and Contribution (110-160 words)

Define the technical task, the baseline or prior limitation, and the claimed
contribution. Specify what is genuinely new according to the paper.

## Formalism and Assumptions (150-230 words)

Define relevant variables, physical or statistical models, hypotheses,
constraints, and applicability conditions. Include reported equations or
relationships only when supported by the source; explain notation precisely.

## Materials, Data, and Experimental Setup (140-220 words)

Describe specimens or datasets, instruments or computational resources,
selection criteria, acquisition conditions, and controls as applicable.
Mark details not reported in the paper rather than supplying defaults.

## Procedure or Algorithm (220-320 words)

Explain the sequence of operations: preprocessing, measurement, calibration,
model fitting or training, inference, and postprocessing as relevant.
Record decisive parameter choices and implementation details when given.

## Validation and Quantitative Results (220-320 words)

Describe evaluation design, baselines, metrics, error estimates, statistical
methods, and numerical results. State which comparisons are supported and
which are not. Distinguish performance on test data from generalization.

## Reproducibility and Failure Boundaries (140-210 words)

Identify reported code/data availability, parameters, uncertainty sources,
known failure modes, and the conditions required to replicate the findings.
Do not infer that unmentioned artifacts are unavailable.

## Technical Assessment and Open Questions (100-150 words)

Summarize what the results establish, what remains unverified, and what a
technically trained reader should check before applying the approach.
Separate the paper's claims from independent analytical inferences.

## Related Reviews and Literature (2–5 items)

The papers or reviews that give readers the best context, not the paper
itself. Chosen from {references}.
