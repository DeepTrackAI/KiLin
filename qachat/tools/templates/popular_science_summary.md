# Headline (max 100 characters)

Write a clear, engaging headline about the actual finding. Avoid hype,
clickbait, and promises of applications not demonstrated, but try to be catchy,
witty, and entertaining. Never use a fullstop at the end of this title.

**Based on the study:** {citation}

## The Puzzle (90-130 words)

Open with a concrete question or familiar phenomenon that leads naturally
to the study. Explain why it is interesting without assuming prior knowledge.

## The Idea Behind the Investigation (90-130 words)

Introduce the central scientific idea and what researchers needed to learn.
Explain indispensable terminology in everyday language; avoid acronyms.

## How They Looked for an Answer (110-160 words)

Tell the story of the experiment, observation, or simulation using concrete,
accurate descriptions. Explain the key comparison in intuitive terms.
Do not use an analogy that changes the mechanism or causal interpretation.

## The Surprising or Important Result (150-210 words)

Explain the main finding and why the evidence is persuasive. Translate
numbers into understandable scale only when that preserves their meaning.
Distinguish observations from interpretations and avoid overstating certainty.

## What We Can and Cannot Conclude (100-150 words)

Clarify the scope of the result, the main uncertainties, and common possible
misreadings. Explicitly distinguish a model-system finding from a claim
about everyday life or human health where relevant.

## The Next Question (60-90 words)

End with a specific open question or testable next step, not a generic
prediction that the discovery will transform society.
